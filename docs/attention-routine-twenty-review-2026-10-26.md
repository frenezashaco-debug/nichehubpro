## Editorial changes

Separated metaphors from biological measurements, task performance from general attention transfer, habit self-report from neural measurements, and self-care from assessment or treatment. Removed universal fuel-tank explanations, fixed willpower cutoffs, claims that every delay has an emotional root, guaranteed effects from deadlines, forced countdowns and morning routines presented as stress or hormone interventions.

Each guide has a distinct authored aid: demand map, evidence checklist, routine usefulness note, deadline assumption sheet, decision information card, task-practice note, neural-evidence boundary card, optional rest-support note, morning contingency, delayed-task map, concentration discussion note, learning session card, first-response choice, thinking-change note, home-work agreement, care-obstacle note, morning preparation list, support routes, first-change suitability card and morning support handoff. Fictional examples do not assert measured outcomes.

## Sources checked and boundaries

- [Hagger et al. (2016), ego-depletion replication](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616652873): the willpower guide reports the protocol-specific estimate from 23 laboratories and 2,141 participants; it does not declare every self-control mechanism settled or assess an individual reserve.
- [Gilbert (2015), intention offloading](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2014.972963): the cognitive-load guide links the exact external-reminder study discussed. Results about delayed intentions are not presented as benefits for every decision or a treatment.
- [Dunlosky et al. (2013), learning techniques](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266): the student guide distinguishes retrieval, feedback and distributed practice from timed concentration; no grade guarantee or universal spacing interval is asserted.
- [NIDCD: Neural plasticity](https://www.nidcd.nih.gov/glossary/neural-plasticity): definition only, not validation of a brain-rewiring programme.
- [NIMH RDoC: Habit](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-funded-by-nimh/rdoc/constructs/habit-pvs): a research construct, not a diagnosis or proof that everyone craves a rigid schedule.
- [Lally et al. (2010), habit formation](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674): self-reported automaticity and substantial variation, not neural imaging or a personal deadline.
- [APA: Switching costs](https://www.apa.org/topics/research/multitasking): limited task-switching background, not evidence for permanent attention training.
- [NIMH: Caring for Your Mental Health](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/caring-for-your-mental-health): general self-care and help-seeking context, not an individual prescription or validation of these worksheets.
- [WHO: Mental health at work](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/mental-health-at-work): work conditions and support, not a performance multiplier.
- [NIDDK: Changing Your Habits for Better Health](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/diet-nutrition/changing-habits-better-health): general roadblock planning, not a universal dose or timeline.
- [GTD: Official overview](https://gettingthingsdone.com/what-is-gtd/): task clarification as a creator-described method, not clinical evidence about procrastination.
- [NHS: Sudden confusion](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/confusion/): the mental-clarity guide distinguishes urgent sudden confusion from a general habit question and avoids delaying needed help for tracking.

NINDS and Nature pages that could not be retrieved were not used to support new claims. The NIA page was not relied upon for a population-wide brain-training conclusion. These editorial aids are not validated research instruments or clinical protocols.
